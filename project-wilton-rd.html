Wilton Road

Belgravia, London 2023 Full interior design

The brief here was deceptively simple: make a Victorian terrace feel genuinely easy to live in, without losing any of its character. No clutter, no fussiness, but no austerity either. The kind of home where everything has been thought about, and none of it shows.

White-painted exposed brick runs through the ground floor, giving the rooms a texture that keeps the all-white palette from feeling cold. The reception is anchored by a three-arm Serge Mouille ceiling light — the room's one statement — and the rest follows its lead: a slate-grey sofa, a pair of terracotta velvet armchairs, a Saarinen tulip table in marble. Floor-to-ceiling open shelving on industrial rails runs the length of one wall, holding books, plants and the accumulation of a family's everyday life. The shelving is deliberately unfussy — storage that earns its place by being visible, not despite it.

The kitchen is where a family actually lives, and this one was designed around that fact. A long, generously proportioned oak dining table seats the whole household and then some, surrounded by matte-black spindle chairs that sit well against the natural grain. The kitchen run itself is clean and hardworking: white handleless cabinetry, stainless steel worktops, open oak shelving above the range for things in daily use. At the garden end, full-height crittall-style doors bring the outside in, and the red perforated steel credenza against the whitewashed brick — topped with a brass gramophone horn and a painting of a kitchen larder — becomes the room's personality in a single corner.

The hallway does exactly what it should: a blush-painted wall, a round mirror with a hair-thin black frame, an oak shelf floating above a column radiator. Three decisions. Nothing more needed.
