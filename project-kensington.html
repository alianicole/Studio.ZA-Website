Kensington

Kensington, London 2018 Full interior design

The unifying idea here was joinery — specifically, what happens when a single material language runs unbroken through an entire flat. Custom fluted oak cabinetry moves from the kitchen island to the living room credenza to the full-height study storage, each piece the same in character but different in function. The repetition creates a calm that lets the stone do the talking.

And the stone does talk. The kitchen takes Calacatta marble from worktop to splashback, the veining continuous across every surface so the whole run reads as a single plane. The island — oversized, heavily grained, with a bullnose edge that softens the mass — is the room's centrepiece; the fluted oak below it has the same reeded profile as every other piece in the flat, but at this scale it becomes architectural.

The cloakroom is where the restraint breaks. Deep green fish-scale tiles cover every wall, and the vanity is carved from a single block of Calacatta Viola — the burgundy and white veining of the stone set against the green in a combination that probably shouldn't work as well as it does. A wall-mounted brass tap in an unusual tulip form was the find that made the room.

Throughout, the hardware — faceted antique brass pulls — does the decorative work that the joinery deliberately avoids. Small moments: the internal lighting that fires up when a study cupboard opens; the pull-out shelves inside the island; the two-tone oak of the tall storage units, fluted above and dark-stained below. The kind of detail you don't notice until you do, and then can't stop noticing.
